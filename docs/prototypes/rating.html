How the strength rating is worked out

One number per exercise, like a chess rating: what you lifted, placed among real people of your build, bodyweight and age, moving a little with every workout. Every number here is the app's own calculation (mobile/lib/logic/rating.dart) on its own standards (data/standards/), for a few sample lifts.

Four steps from a set to a rating

1

What the set is worth

Weight × (1 + reps/30): an estimated one-rep max. A workout averages its sets.

2

The standard for you

Five tiers that beat 5, 20, 50, 80, 95 % of people who train it, at your build, weight and age.

3

Where you sit

Ranked in a mix: three parts people who train, one part everyone.

4

Rating and rank

1200 is the middle; 350 points is one standard deviation.

Who you are compared with

The share of each group a strength beats. People who train run straight through the standard's five tiers (the dots). Everyone else sits a little under their 5 % mark, where studies of untrained adults are. Your place is the mix: 75 % people who train, 25 % everyone.

From strength to rating

That share on the chess-like scale. Steep in the middle, where most people are; flatter at the ends, so past Elite each extra kilogram is worth fewer points.

How it moves, like Elo

The first workout sets it outright. The next three move it half the way to that day's worth, then a quarter. A day that does not count against you can lift it, never lower it. A sample history around your set:

What it considers

  • Weight and reps of every set, averaged over the workout
  • The version of a bodyweight exercise, by the share of bodyweight it moves (knee push-ups 0.49, push-ups 0.64)
  • Your build: men's or women's tables, from the body figure in Settings
  • Your bodyweight: tables every 10 kg, read in proportion between
  • Your age on the day: all of it 20 to 39, then 0.885 at 50, 0.77 at 60, 0.64 at 70
  • How the day went: easy, low energy, light or hurt can lift it, never lower it
  • Your history on the lift: half the way while new, a quarter once settled

What it does not

  • Rest, tempo, effort or form: only what was lifted, how many times
  • Other exercises: each lift has its own; the overall rating averages them
  • Anything stored: worked out from the log every time

What the standards stand on

Measured: lifts logged on strengthlevel.com (plus Symmetric Strength for barbell lifts). Modelled: FitnessVolt's tables only. Close cousin: a similar exercise's page. Estimated: no page; from the beginner start.
